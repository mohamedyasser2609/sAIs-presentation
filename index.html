Operational Context • Problem Statement

Conventional Mall Parking Inefficiencies

Commercial shopping mall parking facilities face critical operational friction, driving customer dissatisfaction, space waste, and internal congestion.

• Search Time & Congestion

Drivers spend 10–25 minutes circulating through parking aisles, creating bottlenecks at main entrances and escalators.

• Inefficient Space Utilization

Conventional facilities require wide door-opening clearance and generous turning buffers, losing up to 30–40% of potential floor capacity.

• Limited Real-Time Visibility

Static signs provide coarse total counts but lack slot-level accuracy, predictive forecasts, and verified reservation capabilities.

• Manual & Disconnected Operations

Traditional valet services rely on human drivers, manual key handling, liability risks, and disconnected digital infrastructure.

Operational Dimension Conventional Mall Parking sAIs Proposed Solution
Customer Experience Manual search, aisle congestion, stress, spot disorientation. Quick drop-off at designated station; automated handling & retrieval.
Space Efficiency Wide bays (door clearance needed); large pedestrian corridors. High-density robotic arrangement without requiring open doors during storage.
Internal Traffic Flow Unregulated customer circulation, pedestrian conflicts. Designated vehicle handover zone; segregated automated AGV corridors.
Data & Analytics Basic loop counter totals; retrospective reporting only. Continuous bay telemetry, demand forecasting, and predictive arrival guidance.
Key Finding: Modern smart mobility demands moving from passive guidance systems to active autonomous handling.
System Design • Core Value Proposition

Introducing sAIs: Autonomous Parking Ecosystem

sAIs fundamentally transforms the parking experience by shifting from helping drivers find parking to an ecosystem that handles the parking process.

System Engineering • Technical Architecture

sAIs Multi-Tier System Architecture

Hierarchical architecture establishing clean boundaries between customer-facing digital services, central management logic, and physical robotic control.

Subsystem 1 • Facility Intelligence

Intelligent Parking Management

Autonomous space allocation and continuous bay-level monitoring replacing manual parking searching with dynamic slot optimization.

01 Real-Time Occupancy Sensing

Integrates ceiling-mounted ultrasonic sensors and overhead vision nodes to confirm bay occupancy status in real time with continuous verification.

02 Vehicle Classification

Optical scans at the drop-off station categorize vehicle dimensions, ground clearance, and estimated wheelbase to assign mathematically compatible bays.

03 Intelligent Space Allocation

Calculates optimal bay assignment based on predicted dwell time, robot travel distance, facility balance, and pre-booked VIP reservation requirements.

04 VIP Reservations

Dedicated fast-turnaround bays allocated near pickup stations for app users with scheduled departure reservations.

INDOOR FACILITY BAY TOPOLOGY // LEVEL B1 LIVE TELEMETRY SIMULATION
Indoor layout allocation maximizes density while segregating human drop-off from robot transit corridors.
Subsystem 2 • Robotic Vehicle Handling

Autonomous Robotic Valet Mechanism

The physical heart of sAIs: ultra-low profile automated guided vehicles engineered to maneuver under passenger cars, securely lift wheels, and transport them safely.

SIX-STAGE VEHICLE HANDLING SEQUENCE
STEP 01
Task Assignment

Robot receives target vehicle ID, pickup bay coordinates, and assigned destination from Fleet Manager.

STEP 02
Transit to Station

Navigates autonomously through designated robotic aisle to vehicle drop-off bay using indoor LiDAR SLAM.

STEP 03
Wheel Alignment

Optical sensors identify exact wheel centerlines and tire ground contact points beneath the vehicle chassis.

STEP 04
Mechanical Lift

Integrated clamping arms cradle all four tires and elevate the vehicle 50–100 mm off the concrete floor.

STEP 05
Transport to Bay

Transports vehicle along calculated collision-free trajectory with active localized obstacle avoidance.

STEP 06
Release & Report

Gently lowers vehicle, disengages clamping arms, confirms bay positioning, and transmits task completion.

Technology Benchmark Reference: Inspired by HL Robotics PARKIE indoor AGV system architecture. Proposed conceptual design; sAIs does not claim procurement or existing platform implementation.

Robotic Coordination • Hybrid Architecture

Hybrid Multi-Robot Fleet Management

Combines global centralized task optimization with autonomous local edge control — avoiding the single-point fragility of pure centralized systems and the chaos of uncoordinated swarms.

Correct Architectural Terminology
"Hybrid Multi-Robot Fleet Management with Centralized Task Allocation and Autonomous Local Control"
Centralized Fleet Management
  • Global task assignment & sequencing
  • Robot availability & health monitoring
  • Priority queueing (VIP / retrievals)
  • Overall parking space allocation
  • Automated battery recharge scheduling
Autonomous Robot-Level Control
  • Onboard LiDAR SLAM localization
  • Real-time dynamic obstacle detection
  • Immediate safety-critical braking
  • Local path tracking & motor control
  • Continuous telemetry feedback to core
Architectural Note: Do not describe this system as a fully decentralized swarm. Fleet scheduling requires deterministic central oversight, while collision safety must remain localized.
CENTRAL SCHEDULER ↔ DISTRIBUTED ROBOT FLEET
Robots retain full local decision capability for emergency stopping and obstacle clearance without network lag.

Subsystem 5 • Modular Growth

Smart Services & Future Expansion Opportunities

An architectural foundation designed for post-baseline modular service expansion, unlocking high-value automated vehicle care while parked.

FUTURE EXPANSION ROADMAP • NOT PART OF INITIAL BASELINE SCOPE
EXP 1 EV Charging Integration

Autonomous robots transport electric vehicles to a shared bank of DC fast chargers once parked. After reaching target charge, the robot relocates the car back to standard storage, eliminating EV bay hogging.

Potential: 4x charger utilization efficiency
EXP 2 Automated Car Wash Bays

Customers opt into an automated exterior cleaning service via the mobile application. The fleet scheduler routes the vehicle through an automated wash tunnel during customer shopping dwell time before return.

Revenue model: Value-added valet service
EXP 3 Security & Telematics

Stationary robotic patrolling and automated perimeter visual inspection. Optical sensors verify tire condition, minor exterior damage, and vehicle security while stored in the facility.

Security: Zero unauthorized physical contact
Scope Clarification: While the Tier 2 Central Platform provides modular interface adapters for these services, they are future expansion concepts and not required deliverables of the planning baseline.
State of the Art • Technology Benchmark

Industry Benchmarks & Case Study

Analyzing commercial autonomous parking robotics to validate technical feasibility while establishing sAIs' architectural differentiation.

Platform Primary Environment Documented Core Capabilities Role in sAIs Project
HL Robotics PARKIE Indoor Facilities Ultra-flat chassis; indoor navigation; wheel-position optical detection; mechanical wheel lifting; external PMS API integration. Primary Benchmark: Validates indoor low-clearance vehicle transportation feasibility.

How sAIs Extends Beyond Standalone Robotics

While commercial platforms like PARKIE demonstrate that robotic vehicle transport is mechanically viable, sAIs designs a comprehensive ecosystem uniting robotic valets with customer mobile booking, predictive AI demand forecasting, and integrated facility space management.

Technology Evaluation Principles
  • Benchmark Only: Platforms serve as technical feasibility references, not confirmed vendor procurement selections.
  • No Fabricated Specs: Performance claims rely strictly on vendor published data; unverified payload limits are avoided.
  • Architectural Independence: sAIs software layers (Fleet Manager, PMS, App, AI) remain modular and platform-agnostic.
